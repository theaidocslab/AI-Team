# Sales Plan — The AI Docs Lab Coach Landing Page Offer

## Executive Summary
The offer itself is strong — willingness to pay and business fit both score 5/5, and the $297-once vs. $300-400/mo-forever comparison is a real, defensible edge. The problem isn't the offer, it's that **almost nobody will land on this page cold and just apply.** Credibility scores the lowest of any factor (2/5): the page makes bold claims (a real AI chatbot, AI-search discoverability, 48-hour delivery) with zero visible proof, to an audience that has very likely already been burned by a funnel platform or a guru pitch. The fix isn't a new headline — it's inserting a low-commitment proof step between "saw the content" and "filled out an 8-field form."

## The Core Fix: Add a Proof Step Before the Application
Right now the funnel is: content → 8-field application. That's too big a jump for a skeptical stranger. The recommended funnel is:

**content → live demo page (talk to a real example, zero commitment) → 8-field application → personal review (24 hrs) → delivery (48 hrs)**

This is the single highest-leverage change identified across all four agents. Everything below is built around making that proof step real and getting the right people to it.

## Audience & Positioning
- **Who:** Coaches/consultants with real client results but a landing page that isn't working (or no page at all) — not total beginners; they already have an offer and a niche.
- **What they're guarding against:** Being sold another version of a funnel platform or guru product that didn't deliver. The page's own "why so cheap" and "already paying for a funnel platform" sections exist because the founder already anticipated this.
- **The wedge competitors aren't using:** built to be found by ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity — not just Google. Lead with this, not with "AI-powered" alone (that phrase is now noise in this market).
- **Pre-validated language:** the application form's own dropdown ("I get traffic but nobody books a call," "My page exists but generates zero leads," "I rely 100% on referrals," etc.) is the founder's already-tested language for this audience's pain — use it verbatim in headlines and hooks rather than inventing new phrasing.

## What To Build: The Live Demo Page
A new, small asset — not a redesign of the application page. A public example landing page (e.g. for a fictional "Sarah Johnson, Business Mindset Coach") with a genuinely working embedded chatbot, no email gate, no form. The one-line framing on it: *"This is what $297 buys. No email required to look around."* This is what lets a skeptical visitor self-qualify before committing to the real application.

## Proof To Capture (in priority order)
1. A real example page shown on screen (or the live demo above).
2. The chatbot answering a real, unscripted question — live, unedited.
3. An actual ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity search surfacing a built page — demonstrated, not claimed.
4. Dr. Eric's face and voice on camera saying he reviews every application personally.
5. Build speed shown (application → live page), not just stated.
6. A visual cost comparison: $300–400+/mo forever vs. $297 once.
7. As soon as real clients exist: name them. The first 1-3 delivered pages become the most valuable content asset this business has.

## Urgency Strategy — Real Scarcity, Not a Countdown Clock
The old "spots" pulsing-dot bar reads as fake urgency, which this specific audience has been burned by before. But there's a genuinely honest urgency lever already built into this business: **Dr. Eric personally reviews every application and personally builds every page himself — no team, no template mill.** That's a real, finite resource (his own hours), not a manufactured one. This section applies techniques from six well-known persuasion figures to make that real constraint felt honestly, instead of inventing fake pressure.

**Rewritten "Why Now":**
> Here's the actual reason to apply this week instead of "someday": I build every single page myself — no team, no template farm, no subcontractor. That's not a sales line, it's how the business runs, which means my available hours are the real constraint, not a marketing trick. Every week you wait is a week you're still writing a check to a funnel platform for $97–297/mo plus a separate $97/mo chatbot add-on — $300–400+ a month, forever — for a page that ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity still can't find. Meanwhile the coach who applied this week already has a page live, taking bookings, and showing up when someone asks an AI assistant to find a coach like them. I read every application myself within 24 hours, and I only confirm the ones I genuinely believe will convert — if I don't, I tell you and refund you on the spot. That's the honest limit here: my time and my standards, not a countdown clock.

**Replacement for the pulsing "spots" bar:** kill the pulsing dot — animated UI urgency reads as automated even when the words are honest. Two options, ranked by what's safe to publish today:

1. **Safe to publish now (no number needed):** *"Every page is reviewed and built by Dr. Eric personally — not a team, not outsourced. That's why applications go through a real review instead of instant checkout, and why turnaround is 48 hours from confirmation, not same-day."*
2. **Stronger, but needs your input first — a real "Build Queue" line:** *"BUILD QUEUE — Week of [date]: I personally review and build every page myself, which means I can only take on [X] new builds per week. Apply now to be considered for this week's queue."* **This needs a real number from you** — how many 48-hour builds can you actually complete in a week, doing the copy, chatbot training, and build yourself? Don't publish a guessed number; it's fine for some weeks to just say "queue is open" with no number.

**Which technique came from which name:**
- **Edward Bernays** — urgency from Dr. Eric's own demonstrated authority (he personally reviews and builds), not manufactured crowd pressure.
- **Dan Kennedy** — the "reason why" is stated outright in the same breath as the ask, never left as unsupported hype.
- **Russell Brunson** — cost-of-inaction is quantified in real terms the reader already pays ($300–400/mo, invisible to AI search), not a fake deadline.
- **MrBeast** — the urgency claim never stands alone; it sits right next to visible proof of it ("no team, no template mill, nobody outsourced").
- **Marion Harper Jr.** — urgency language uses the application form's own pre-tested pain phrases, aimed at this exact buyer, not a generic "spots are limited" line.
- **Gary Halbert** — short, blunt, unadorned sentences carry the whole argument, with the refund guarantee stacked right next to the urgency claim.

## Risks & Friction To Watch
- **The demo must be clearly labeled as an example**, not "your" page, or visitors may think it's a bait-and-switch.
- **Follow-up emails must stay reassurance-first**, not drip-campaign-pushy — this audience will recognize an automated sales cadence instantly and it will re-trigger the exact distrust the funnel is trying to remove.
- **The demo has to be genuinely fast and ungated** — if it's slow or feels canned, it damages trust worse than not having a demo at all.
- **Don't send paid/cold traffic straight at the application** — route it through content and the demo first; the 8-field form converts a warmed visitor, not a cold one.
